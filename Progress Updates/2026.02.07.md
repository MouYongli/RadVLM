### Interpretation of results
- **Strong base-model advantage for MedGemma**
	- MedGemma base model already outperforms all DeepSeek variants across most metrics.
	- This is expected, as MedGemma is pretrained specifically for radiology report generation, while DeepSeek is a more general vision–language model.
	- The large gap in RadGraph-F1 indicates that MedGemma encodes more clinically meaningful structure.

- **DeepSeek improves substantially after pretraining**
	- RadGraph-F1 (complete) increases from **0.0349 → ~0.09–0.096** on the small dataset.
	- This corresponds to roughly a **2.5–3× improvement** in clinical correctness over the base model.
	- Cross-entropy and perplexity also drop significantly.

- **Scaling data improves NLG metrics but not clinical correctness**
	- Moving from 1,405 to 14,054 samples:
		- BLEU and ROUGE consistently increase.
		- RadGraph-F1 slightly decreases for DeepSeek.
	- This suggests the model is learning surface-level text patterns more effectively than clinical relationships.
	- Possible causes:
		- LoRA is only applied to cross-modal attention modules and language model.

- **Effect of LoRA rank**
	- For DeepSeek on the small dataset:
		- r=32 slightly improves NLG metrics.
		- Clinical metrics remain similar across ranks.
	- On the large dataset:
		- r=8 performs slightly better clinically than r=32.
	- This may indicate that:
		- Higher ranks increase capacity for stylistic adaptation.
		- Lower ranks regularize the model and preserve clinical reasoning.

- **Scheduler and warmup effects**
	- Linear schedulers with small warmup ratios (≈5–10%) consistently outperform cosine schedules.
	- Very large warmup ratios or longer training (5 epochs) tend to reduce performance, suggesting:
		- The dataset is relatively small.
		- The model quickly reaches its optimal region.

- **MedGemma benefits strongly from scaling**
	- With the full dataset:
		- RadGraph-F1 (complete) increases from **0.1721 → 0.2096**.
		- BLEU-4 nearly doubles compared to the base model.
	- Unlike DeepSeek, MedGemma improves in both:
		- Surface-level NLG metrics
		- Clinical correctness metrics
	- This indicates that:
		- The model’s inductive bias is already aligned with the task.
		- Additional data is effectively used for clinical refinement.

- **Qualitative observations**
	- DeepSeek outputs remain repetitive and template-like.
	- MedGemma produces more varied and clinically structured reports.

- **Best hyperparameters for DeepSeek (based on RadGraph results)**
	- LoRA rank: 16–32
	- Learning rate: 1e-4
	- Number of epochs: 3
	- Scheduler type: linear
	- Warmup ratio: 5%

- **Overall conclusion**
	- MedGemma is currently the stronger and more stable base model for this task.
	- DeepSeek can be improved substantially with pretraining but still lags behind in clinical correctness.
	- Future gains for DeepSeek will likely be achieved by DPO training.
